# TrackID Community Guidelines

**Effective:** 7 August 2026
**Last updated:** 10 August 2026

TrackID exists to do one thing well: help people find out what that track was. It works
because strangers take the time to answer, and that only continues while this is a place
people want to spend time in.

These rules are short on purpose. They apply to everything you post — clips, track
suggestions, usernames, bios and avatars.

---

## The one-line version

Post clips of music you want identified. Help other people find theirs. Do not film people
who did not want to be filmed, and be decent to the people in the threads.

---

## What belongs here

- Clips of a set where you want to know the track
- Genuine attempts to identify someone else's clip, including honest guesses labelled as
  guesses
- Votes on suggestions you actually believe are right

## What does not

**Content that is not about the music.** TrackID is not a general video app. Clips that
are not attempts to identify a track will be removed.

**Sexual content and nudity.** No explicit content, no sexualised content involving anyone
who is or appears to be a minor — the latter gets reported to the authorities, not just
deleted.

**Violence and dangerous behaviour.** No fights, no graphic injury, no glorifying
self-harm. Nightlife includes drug use; do not post content that promotes or instructs on
it. A clip that happens to include a crowd is fine. A clip that is about the drugs is not.

**Harassment and hate.** No targeting individuals, no slurs, no attacks based on race,
ethnicity, national origin, religion, disability, age, sex, gender identity or sexual
orientation. Disagreeing about a track ID is normal. Going after the person is not.

**Filming people who did not agree to it.** This rule is specific to what TrackID does, and
it matters more here than most. Point the camera at the stage, the crowd generally, or your
own group. Do not post
a clip that focuses on an identifiable person who plainly did not want to be recorded, and
do not post one to mock someone. If someone asks us to remove a clip they appear in, we
will usually remove it — and being unhappy about that is not grounds for reposting it.

**Filming where you were told not to.** Some venues and artists prohibit recording. Where
recording was prohibited, do not post the clip here — whether or not the restriction was
enforced on the night.

**Content you have no right to post.** Do not upload full recorded sets, official releases,
professionally produced footage, or anyone else's clips reposted as your own. Short clips
for identification are the format; a full copy of a set is not.

**Gaming the system.** No deliberately wrong suggestions, no upvoting yourself with a second
account, no multiple accounts to swing votes, no spam, no promoting your own releases through
fake IDs. Points exist to recognise people who actually help. Inflating them steals from
everyone who earned theirs.

**Impersonation.** Do not pretend to be a DJ, a label, a venue, or another user.

## Reporting something

If you see content that breaks these rules, report it from inside the app:

- **A clip** — open it and tap the **…** button in the top right, then *Report this clip*.
- **A track suggestion** — tap the small flag icon on the suggestion in the thread.

Pick the reason that fits and add a note if there is something we would not work out on our
own. Reports are private: the person you report is never told who reported them.

We aim to review every report **within 24 hours**, and faster for anything involving a
minor, a credible threat, or someone's safety. We do not tell you what action we took against
a specific account, but we will confirm we have dealt with it.

You can also email ewan.nicol55@gmail.com — use that if you cannot reach the content in the app,
or if the problem is an account rather than one thing it posted.

If a clip shows you and you want it gone, say so. You do not need to point to a rule, and you
do not need a TrackID account to ask.

---

## Blocking someone

If a specific person is making TrackID worse for you, block them. Tap **Block** on their
profile, or use the **…** button on any of their clips.

Once blocked, you stop seeing their clips and suggestions everywhere in the app — Discover,
search, scene feeds, threads — and they stop seeing yours. Any friendship between you is
removed. They are not told. You can undo it any time in **Settings → Blocked accounts**, or
from their profile.

Blocking is not a report. It fixes the problem for you and nobody else, so if someone broke a
rule, report them as well — otherwise the next person meets the same behaviour.

---

## What happens when a rule is broken

We aim for the smallest action that fixes the problem:

- **Content removed** — for a one-off that looks like a misjudgement
- **Warning** — for a first offence that was clearly deliberate
- **Points reset** — where the points were the motive, as with vote manipulation
- **Suspension** — for repeat offences, or a single serious one
- **Permanent ban** — for sexual content involving minors, credible threats, or sustained
  harassment. No warnings, no second account.

Bans apply to the person, not the account. Making a new one to get around a ban is itself
grounds for banning it.

**Appeals.** If you believe a decision was wrong, reply to the email we sent you, or write to
ewan.nicol55@gmail.com, setting out what you think we misread. The decision will be reviewed
by a person, and we do reverse decisions that were wrong.

---

## The spirit of these rules

Almost nobody removed from a community like this set out to break a rule. More often they
became competitive about points, lost patience during an argument, or posted a clip of a
friend that was funnier to them than it was to the person in it.

Two questions are worth asking before you post. Would you be happy for the person in the clip
to see it? And if you are about to reply sharply to someone, is the track identification
really what you are disagreeing about?
